Abstract—Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has
shown great success in many aspects. However, the cooperative
policy trained by MARL is vulnerable to adversarial attacks
towards agents’ observations, which could cause immeasurable
damage to the agent team. A few techniques have been de-
veloped to test the robustness of MARL, but the feasibility of
real implementation is not carefully considered. In this work,
we propose a two-step framework to conduct destructive and
sparse attacks under realistic conditions. The first step contains
two attack scenes: Ally Observation Attack (AOA) and Enemy
Observation Attack (EOA), which have limitations on the use
of agents’ observations. The first step selects victim agent from
the team and screens the attack time, while the second step
generates adversarial perturbation and adds it to the observation
of the chosen victim in a black-box environment. To the best
of our knowledge, this is the first work to test the robustness
with partial observation and conduct the test in a black-box
environment. Experiments on SMAC environments demonstrate
that our methods show great performance in reducing the win
rate and the team reward of the agent team trained by QMIX
algorithm with lower perturbation steps.
Index Terms—multi-agent reinforcement learning, black box
attack, adversarial attack, robustness testing, partial observation.
I. INTRODUCTION
Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has achieved
remarkable success in a wide range of real-world applications,
including unmanned aerial vehicle control [1], autonomous
driving [2] and traffic management [3]. As MARL algorithms
play a increasingly critical role in practical scenarios, the
robustness of them desires to be carefully tested. Agents
trained under classical MARL algorithms are found vulnerable
to adversarial attacks towards agents’ observations. Suffering
from such adversarial attacks, agents would choose actions vi-
olate their origin strategies, which could cause huge damage to
the team. In cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning
(c-MARL), the cooperation between agents are learned during
training time and can help the team better complete tasks.
However, the performance of the team is easier to be affected
when agents in the team suffer attacks. Testing robustness of
c-MARL is an urgent task need to be solved.

To better test the robustness of models trained by MARL
algorithms, we believe that an ideal testing framework should
not only be highly destructive to cause more damage to the
team performance, but also stealthy and practical, meaning
that it should require minimal perturbation steps and operate
under realistic implementing conditions. Unfortunately, many
existing robustness testing methods for MARL ignore the
realistic conditions, which limit their practical applicability.
If the required conditions could help methods perform well
but are not easy to achieve in real situations, we would not
find a proper way to maintain the robustness of models trained
by MARL algorithms.
There exists many techniques for testing robustness of
reinforcement learning algorithms [4], [5], but only a few of
them discuss about multi-agent environments. According to
the victims of the attacking methods, the robustness testing
methods for MARL could be classified into two types. The
first kind of methods attack one determined agent in the
team [6]. Attacking one determined agent could achieve good
performance when the environment is simple, but it could
face bottleneck when environments become more complex.
Therefore, the second kind of methods, which attack multiple
agents or change the attacking victims over time, is the key to
test robustness [7]–[9]. However, there are still some problems
need to be solved in existing methods:
• The adversary often uses MARL algorithms or differen-
tial evolution to select the victim agent and the attack
time, which requires much prior knowledge about the
agent team, training before the attack, or doing real-
time computation during attacks. Due to these reasons,
the adversary need to retrain or recompute when the
environments get changed, and the immediacy of the
method is also under trial.
• Carrying out existing methods often requires the access
to all agents’ observations, which is not always realistic
in real implementation. There is an urgent need for the
enhancement of practicality of testing methods.
• Some methods assume the adversary can change the
actions of agents instead of using adversarial attacks

to perturb the observations. The other methods using
adversarial attacks need the parameters of the model.
Both are often hard to implement in reality.
To bridge this gap between methodological rigor and practi-
cal implementability, we propose a novel two-step framework
to conduct destructive and sparse attacks under realistic con-
ditions. In the first step, we design two attack scenes: Ally
Observation Attack (AOA) and Enemy Observation Attack
(EOA), which simulates the real scenario with strict limitations
on the use of agents’ observations. Based on two different
scenes, the first step is to identify a suitable victim agent and
determines an optimal timing for the attack based on limited
available information. In the second step, we propose black-
box methods to generate adversarial perturbations, which will
be added to the observations of the selected victim agent at
the chosen time step. To the best of our knowledge, this is
the first work to systematically address black-box adversarial
robustness testing in MARL under strict partial observation
constraints. What’s more, the observations used to guide
attacks are the features of the MARL environment, which
improve the performance of our method. Our contributions
are concluded as follows:
• This paper proposes a two-step attack method which is
easier to implement and closer to real-world applications.
The first step is to select the victim agent and screen the
attack time through partial observation of the agent in the
team or partial observation of the enemy. The second step
is to add perturbations to the observation of the victim
agent through an extended black-box adversarial attack
method.
• We focus on the practicality of method implementa-
tion. In addition to minimize the requirements for the
observations of agents, we consider both query attacks
and substitute model attacks for black-box adversarial
perturbations generation.
• We carry out a variety of experiments to show that our
method can greatly reduce the win rate and team rewards
of the agent team trained by QMIX [10] with lower
perturbation steps. We also verify the effectiveness of
the components of the proposed method through ablation
study. Our method combines stealthiness and destructive-
ness, and is more realistic in implementation, making
it suitable for robustness testing of models trained by
MARL algorithms.

A. CTDE and QMIX
Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE)
[11], [13] is a leading framework in MARL. In this paradigm,
agents leverage shared information during a central training
phase to develop coordinated strategies. During execution,
however, each agent takes actions independently based solely
on its own local observations. This effectively combines the
advantages of centralized learning with the practical require-
ments of decentralized deployment.
QMIX [10] represents a successful instantiation of CTDE
framework, addressing limitations of earlier value decompo-
sition methods such as VDN [13]. As a value-based MARL
algorithm, QMIX integrates concepts from Actor Critic [15]
and DQN [16], [17]. Using the CTDE framework, QMIX
utilizes a centralized mixing network to receive global states
to guide agents in updating their own parameters. Each agent
in QMIX is controlled by a DRQN [18], which is a DQN

B. Adversarial Attacks
Adversarial example aims at adding small perturbations to
the model inputs to lead the model give wrong classifications
to the inputs at a high confidence. It was first defined by [19].
Adversarial attacks could be divided into two types: the white
box attack and the black-box attack.
In the white box attack, the adversary needs sufficient access
to the model parameters and gradients to launch an attack.
Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) [20] is a classical white
box attack, which uses the gradients of loss functions on the
inputs to quickly generate adversarial perturbations. Besides
FGSM, Project Gradient Descent (PGD) [21] and the Basic
Iterative Method (BIM) [22] also use the gradients to launch
attacks. C&W [23] is another white box attack which generates
adversarial examples based on optimization, while DeepFool
[24] is a boundary based attack. In the black-box attack, an
adversary does not need to know about the model structures
or gradients, attack could be implemented by querying the
model or using the transferability of adversarial examples.
Query-based methods could generate adversarial examples by
estimating the gradients such as ZOO [25] and AutoZOOM
[26], or find the decision boundary of the model such as
the Boundary Attack [27]. Methods such as [28] consider
about improving the transferability of adversarial examples
generated by local surrogate models.
In robustness testing for MARL algorithms, we value the
immediacy of attacking methods, so we will choose the
methods that could quickly generate adversarial examples such
as FGSM.
C. Robustness Testing Methods for MARL
While numerous studies have explored robustness testing for
single-agent reinforcement learning [4], [5], [31], fewer have
addressed the multi-agent setting. Early work which applied
adversarial attacks on MARL algorithms to test robustness
such as [6] only attack one determined agent in the team,
which might be limited in performance when environments
become more complex. Other work likes [33], [34] which at-
tacks on multiple determined agents need retrain the adversary
when victims changed.
Recently, some work such as [8], [9], [40] use a MARL
algorithm or differential evolution to select victim agents from
the team, and the victims could be multiple and changed
by time steps. Those techniques could better test the MARL
algorithm for they could cause huge damage to the team. How-
ever their methods for selecting victims and corresponding
actions would harm the immediacy of implementation. There
are some methods such as [7], [35] use adversarial examples to
attack the state of agents with no specific target. However, the
appropriate limitations of using agents’ observations as well
as the difficulties of launching such a white box attack have
not been carefully considered yet. Generally, the adversary
does not have the observations of all agents in the beginning
and should not have too much information of the agent model
before game. Testing methods should be more realistic in the
implementation conditions to reasonably improve the MARL
algorithms. Therefore, we try to present a black-box testing
method with realistic conditions in this paper.
III. BLACK-BOX ATTACKS WITH PARTIAL OBSERVATION
A. Problem Formulation
A c-MARL game could be modeled as decentralized par-
tially observable Markov process (Dec-POMDP), which could
be defined as tuple ⟨N, S, A, T, R, O, Z, γ⟩, where N is the
number of agents,γ is discount factor, S is the state space
includes the information of environment and agents. A =
[A1, A2, . . . , An] is the action space of the joint actions, at
each time step, agenti will choose ai ∈Ai, and the envi-
ronment will transit to next state according to state transition
function T (s′ | s, a) : S×A×S →[0, 1]. Observation function
Z : S ×A →O determines the observations oi ∈O which are
drawn by each agent individually at each time step, and then
all agents will get the same immediate reward r defined by the
shared team reward function R : S ×A×S →R. The purpose
of the agent team is to find optimal actions for each agent at
each time step to maximize the team reward. Therefore, if we
apply adversarial attacks on MARL algorithms, we need to
figure out which agent to attack and when to attack first.
In this paper, We propose a two-step attack framework. The
first step is to select an agent from the agent team as the
victim and screen a specific time step as the attack time. The
second step is to generate adversarial perturbations and add
them to the victim’s observation, causing the actions of the
victim agent to be misled and causing damage to the team.
The two-step attack framework is shown as Fig. 2.
B. Select the Victim Agent and the Attack Time
The purpose of the first attack step is to select the victim
agent for adding adversarial perturbations in the next step as
well as screen the attack time. In order to make the attacks
possess stealthiness and destructiveness and be implementable,
the algorithm requires to seek the following properties:
• Minimize the number of times to attack.

As discussed in the last section, existing methods for testing
robustness require a large amount of computations and the
observations of all agents. Some methods using adversarial
attacks even need the model parameters. Those are difficult
to achieve in reality. The method proposed in this paper aims
to use the properties of MARL itself to select the optimal
attack time and victim agents. Therefore, we have designed
the following two scenes.
1) Ally Observation Attack: It is very likely to happen
that the adversary cannot get the observations of all agents
in the beginning of the game. Thus the adversary needs a
medium to gradually gain more information of the agent team.
In order to sparingly and reasonably obtaining and using the
observations of agents, one possible scenario is that we can
obtain the partial observation of an single agent in the team,
which is named as Ally Observation Attack (AOA). Since we
only require the observation of the single agent without further
information of the model parameters or policy, the implement
conditions of this scenario is relatively easy to satisfy. Then,
based on the observation values of this agent, we can gradually
know about other agents appearing in the observation, thereby
selecting the victim agent from them. The main idea is that
we can not get any information of an agent or attack it unless
we have observed it. Noted that the adversary cannot avoid
using the observation of the corresponding agent to generate
adversarial perturbations, such observation may be constructed
by inference. The victim agent selecting method of AOA is
shown in Fig. 3.
By using the partial observation ot of a single agent agentt
from the team, we can get access to {agenti | agenti in ot},
which are the other ally agents observed in this observation. By
selecting one agent out of them as the victim agent agentv, the
adversary can add adversarial perturbations to the observation
of this victim agent. This attack can lead the victim agent
take actions that contradict to its original strategy, which
could break the cooperation between the agents and cause a
devastating blow to the team. To be more specific, we select
the victim agent from the observed agents by comparing the
difference between the Q-values before and after being added

IV. EXPERIMENTS
A. Experiment Settings
1) Environment Settings: We choose StarCraftII Multi-
Agent Challenge (SMAC) [14] to evaluate our methods.
SMAC is a complex environment based on the popular real-
time strategy game StarCraft II. In SMAC, the agent team
needs to control a group of agents to defeat enemies controlled
by computers in a highly dynamic environment, which is
a huge test to the cooperation between agents. There are
different maps in SMAC, each map has different numbers of
agents to control as well as different number of enemies to
defeat. In order to cover diverse cases, we choose two maps,
i.e., 2s3z and MMM, to conduct experiments. In 2s3z map,
there are 2 Stalkers and 3 Zealots in the agent team and the
enemy team. In MMM map, there are 1 Medivac, 2 Marauders
and 7 Marines in the agent team. Thus 2s3z is a simpler
map compared to MMM, which could help us further discuss
the performance and characteristics of the robustness testing
methods.

in SMAC environments is evaluated by the following metrics:
the win rate (WR), the death number of enemies (DE), the
death number of allies (DA) and the team reward (TR). We
also use the perturbation steps (PS) to evaluate the stealthiness
of methods in experiments, which means the number of steps
that adversary adds perturbations to the observations. In each

is that the EOA method only launches attacks when agents are
observed by the certain enemy, which makes the fault-tolerant
badly reduced.
D. Experiment Results of Generating Adversarial Perturba-
tions
In this part, we test the method of generating adversarial
perturbations by using substitute models on the AOA method.
In particular, we assume that the adversary could only query
the inputs and the corresponding outputs of the model of
agent0, and the adversary could only calculate ∆Q and gen-
erate adversarial perturbations by inputting the observations of
corresponding agents into the model of agent0.
We use “sAOA” to refer the method using substitute models.
The results are shown in Fig. 8. Since performance of the AOA
method nearly reaches convergence at ϵ = 0.3, we only test
the situations with ϵ = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3.
The performance of the attack method using substitute mod-
els is similar to the origin method (i.e., the query based black-
box adversarial attack), and they achieve almost the same
performance in MMM map. Relatively, such method does not
perform so well in simple environment. It is probably because
in environments with more agents and higher dimensions,
agents trained under the same MARL algorithm are more
similar to each other, which makes them easier to suffer from
adversarial perturbations generated by substitute models.
V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose a black-box adversarial robustness
testing method for the model trained by MARL algorithms
with partial observation. Our testing method use a two-step
framework to launch attacks. The first step is to select one
victim agent from the agent team, and the second step is to
generate adversarial perturbations and add them to the victim’s
observation. We propose two algorithms, i.e., AOA and EOA,
for the first step. They properly use the observation of a single
agent or enemy to select victim agents. We present a black-box
adversarial perturbations generation method in the second step,
and explore to attack by substitute models. The results clearly
show the great performance of our methods and indicate the
importance of testing robustness for MARL algorithm with
realistic implementation.
