Optimal Bi-Level Bidding and Dispatching Strategy
Between Active Distribution Network and Virtual
Alliances Using Distributed Robust Multi-Agent
Deep Reinforcement Learning
Ziqing Zhu

Shiwei Xia

Abstract—The deregulated active distribution network (ADN)
would incorporate numerous autonomous stakeholders, including
some emerging distributed virtual alliances (DVAs) like vir-
tual microgrids and virtual power plants. Those DVAs would
autonomously participate in energy market trading through
bidding among themselves and dispatching conducted by the
ADN. In this paper, the optimal bidding and dispatching model
for DVAs and ADN in the day-ahead market is ﬁrst developed as a
stochastic dynamic programming model with the risk of miscon-
duct considered, and then re-formulated as a Markov Decision
Process to be solved by a new Distributed Robust Multi-Agent
Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient algorithm based on the con-
cept of robust Nash equilibrium (RNE). This algorithm is a fully
distributed online optimization that would converge to RNE. It
is an effective risk-averse method to obtain the optimal bid-
ding strategies of DVAs and the optimal dispatching decisions
of distribution system operator (DSO). Its high computational
performance is demonstrated in the case studies, and the strategic
decisions of DVAs and DSO are thoroughly analyzed.
Index Terms—Active distribution network, distributed virtual
alliance, bidding strategy, reinforcement learning.

I. INTRODUCTION
A. Background and Motivation
T
HE DEVELOPMENT of distribution-level market [1]
is an emerging paradigm, with applications in both
academia [2]–[4] and industry [5], to facilitate the eco-
nomic energy management of increasing distributed energy
resources
(DERs).
Particularly,
the
distributed
virtual
alliances (DVAs), including virtual microgrids (VMGs) [6]
and virtual power plants (VPPs) [7]–[8], who serve as
the aggregation of DERs, are autonomously participating
in the market trading. In this emerging paradigm, DVAs
are required to submit their available capacity and bidding
price to the distribution system operator (DSO), who will
perform the optimal dispatching and then determine the
market clearing price (MCP). However, the uncertainty of
renewable distributed generation (RDG) and load demand
of DVAs render the prediction error of net load, which not
only leads to the potential costs for real-time balancing, but
also complicates: 1) the market regulation design to deal
with the deviation between the day-ahead (DA) appointment
and real-time (RT) actual energy procurement; 2) the energy
schedule (including bidding decisions and energy dispatch)
of DVAs and the DSO. This paper aims to tackle these issues
by proposing a novel DA-RT coordination scheme designed
to address the aforementioned uncertainties and investigate
the bi-level bidding and dispatching interaction among DVAs
and the DSO under such a scheme. Speciﬁcally, motivations
of these investigations are explained as follows:
1) By investigating the dynamic bidding behaviors and
optimal bidding strategies of DVAs under new market regimes:
• DVAs can precisely estimate the equilibrium, as elabo-
rated in Section III, of MCP and submit the bidding price
slightly lower or higher than the equilibrium in order to
maximize the proﬁt. Note that DVAs are under a pressing
need to estimate the MCP, especially at the early stage
of market operation with a lack of historical data.
• DSO can ﬁnd potential irregular behaviors of DVAs
caused by improper market regulations and/or nega-
tively contribute to the maximization of social welfare,
as analyzed in Section IV, by simulating the bid-
ding procedure of DVAs, as elaborated in Section III,
before implementing any new market rules to guaran-
tee the fairness of trading and effectiveness of mar-
ket operation based on the incentive compatibility
principle.
• These methodologies will provide paradigmatic guidance
for market participants and operators in other emerging
market regimes.
2) By investigating the DVA-DSO bidding and dispatching
interactions under uncertainties:
• The determination of MCP equilibrium will be more
precise if the dispatching (market clearing) method is
modeled in a more practical and detailed manner, because
bidding and dispatching are always modeled as the
upper-level and lower-level of a hierarchical program-
ming problem, as elaborated in Section II, and will have
signiﬁcant impact on each other.
• The impact of uncertainties can be considered in DVAs’
and DSO’s decisions to improve the modeling accuracy
and indicate their relatively conservative strategies against
the risk in the early stage of market operation. As a result,
the decision of both DVAs and the DSO will be risk-
averse, and the potential ﬁnancial losses caused by the
uncertainty of DVAs’ net load can be mitigated.
B. Literature Review
A considerable amount of literature has been published on
downstream DVA dispatching in the ADN with uncertain-
ties. In [9], a bi-level robust economic dispatch model for
microgrids (MGs) in the ADN was proposed, in which the
upper level is a two-stage robust economic dispatch model of
ADN, and the self-dispatch of MGs based on MCP prediction
is formulated in the lower level. A similar bi-level dispatch-
ing model between VPP and ADN was developed in [10],
and solved by using Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) optimality
conditions, Fortuny-Amat transformation and the strong dual-
ity theorem. The demand response (DR) was incorporated
into ADN in [11] and [12] with participation of MGs, while
multiple objective functions were simultaneously considered.
However, these works are based on mathematical program-
ming methods to re-formulate the problem to a single-level
convex optimization model. Hence, the decision making is
static and cannot respond to change of conditions, such as
variation of available capacities, RDG outputs, load demand.
As a remedy, model prediction control (MPC) was adopted
in [13] and [14], in which accurate net load prediction is
required but hard to archive in practice.
With the emergence of AI techniques, Reinforcement
Learning (RL) in the multi-agent interaction environment pro-
vides potential solutions for dynamic bidding simulation and
market equilibrium computation. However, algorithms, such
as conventional model-based method [15], Q-learning [16],
WoLF-PHC
algorithm [17],

action space. Hence, for scalability, the “curse of dimen-
sionality” [18] will render such algorithms intractable. In
addition, the conventional model-based RL requires detailed
modeling of environment, which is not applicable considering
the uncertainty of both the reward function and state transition
probability.

the bidding information of other DVAs, which would not be

C. Main Contributions and Paper Structure
An interactive and risk-averse bidding and dispatching strat-
egy is proposed in this paper to allow both DVAs and the DSO
to dynamically optimize their strategy under a novel DA-RT
coordination regime. This interactive bi-level dynamic pro-
gramming model is solved by a new algorithm, Distributed
Robust Multi-Agent (DRMA) DDPG, enabling the fully dis-
tributed and risk-averse training of each autonomous stake-
holder involved. This work not only contributes to the optimal
decision for emerging distributed stakeholders but also facili-
tates the behavior simulation and equilibrium computation of
new market paradigms. The main contributions of this paper
are summarized as follows:
• A paradigm for DA and RT market coordination with
DVAs is proposed. In this paradigm, the DSO will be
responsible for organizing the DA market trading, and
DVAs will be penalized for misconducting the appointed
energy procured by DSO in the DA market. Under this
regime, both the DSO and DVAs should consider the
net load prediction error and the resulting penalties in
DA bidding and dispatching.
• A
bi-level
optimal
bidding
and
dispatching
strat-
egy (OBDS) model for the decision making of DVAs and
DSO is developed. In this model, each DVA would con-
tinuously modify its strategy based on the market clearing
results while the DSO would also modify the dispatching
strategy based on the bids submitted by DVAs. The uncer-
tainty of DVA net load is considered in the decisions of
both DVAs and DSO.
• A new DRMA-DDPG algorithm is proposed to solve
the OBDS model. This algorithm is a multi-agent-based
fully-distributed deep reinforcement learning method
enabling both DVAs and DSO to modify their strate-
gies without requiring any information from the oth-
ers. The potential ﬁnancial losses caused by uncertainty
are mitigated by obtaining the so-called robust Nash
equilibrium (RNE).
The remaining parts of this paper are organized as follows. In
Section II, the market paradigm for DVA-ADN interaction and
formulation of the OBDS model is elaborated. In Section III,
the DRMA-DDPG algorithm is proposed to solve the OBDS
model. The comparison of computational performance between
the proposed algorithm and baselines and the analysis of deci-
sions of each stakeholder are presented in Section IV. Finally,
conclusions and future work are given in Section V.
II. PROBLEM FORMULATION
A. Market Paradigm and Model Description
In the proposed DA market paradigm, the downstream
consumers, Load Aggregator (LA), will submit their energy
demand to DSO while the producers, DVAs including the
Controllable Distributed Generators (CDGs) and VPPs, will
submit their available capacity and bidding price to the DSO
based on their prediction of generation output and bidding
strategies. The prosumers, DVAs like VMGs will decide to
sell or purchase energy based on their net load conditions.
The DSO will ﬁnally clear the market while satisfying the
demand of LAs and VMGs. The novelty of this market
paradigm is the penalty for DVAs due to their misconduct-
ing of day-ahead energy procurement. Such penalty has been
deployed in some transmission-level markets, including some
European countries, such as France, Italy, Germany, Spain,
etc. [27], and the Midwest ISO (MISO) [28], However, to the
best of the authors’ knowledge, no literature has incorporated
such regulation to the distribution-level market participants
(VMGs, VPPs, etc.). The rationale behind this setting include:
1) to compensate for the real-time balancing cost [27] caused
by prediction errors made by the DVAs; 2) to serve as an
incentive [28] for DVAs to improve the accuracy of prediction
which may effectively mitigate the real-time imbalance, with
beneﬁt to network secure operation. Hence, DVAs need to con-
sider the implication of such penalties before making their
bidding decisions. After being allocated with appointment of
energy deployment in the DA market clearing, DVAs need to
schedule its energy for RT operation to mitigate the difference
between the DA appointment and RT actual procurement.
Compared with existing bi-level dispatching models, the
novelties of the proposed OBDS model lies in the follow-
ing aspects. Firstly, the optimal dynamic bidding strategies
and market clearing strategies are simultaneously considered.
DVAs would continuously modify their bidding prices and
available capacity based on previous market clearing results.
Meanwhile, DSO would also modify the market clearing
results based on the updated bidding price and the avail-
able capacity provided by DVAs. Secondly, different types
of uncertainties are considered in formulating the strategies

III. DISTRIBUTED ROBUST MULTI-AGENT DEEP
DETERMINISTIC POLICY GRADIENT (DRMA-DDPG)
ALGORITHM CONSIDERING UNCERTAINTIES
In this section, the proposed DRMA-DDPG algorithm is
introduced by the following steps. Firstly, the framework of
DDPG is elaborated as the baseline to be further enhanced.
After the baseline DDPG being extended to the multi-agent
environment, the concept of Robust Nash Equilibrium (RNE)
is incorporated into the formulation of policy gradient and
value function, enabling this algorithm to be risk-averse. Then,
the training method of this algorithm is improved to be fully
distributed, in order to facilitate the privacy protection. Finally,
tricks are introduced to tackle the limitations of this proposed
algorithm.
A. Framework of the Baseline DDPG Algorithm
As indicated in Section II-C, the status and actions of both
DVAs and DSO are all about market operating data, which
are continuous in real-world applications. Hence, one of the
most important problems to be tackled in this market bid-
ding problem is the continuous state and action space, i.e., the
dimensionality can be considered as inﬁnity, which will render
the convergence of the RL algorithm intractable. Intuitively,
to tackle these problems, the proposed algorithm is developed
based on the DDPG because of its following characteristics:
1) The “policy gradient” (PG) method intends to optimize
the parameterized policy function using the deep neural
network, which makes it applicable to consider the con-
tinuous state and action space and tackles the “curse of
dimensionality”.
2) The “deterministic” PG, compared with the “stochas-

space with large dimensionality after the policy func-

D. Analysis of DSO Dispatching Strategy
In this section, the dispatching results generated by optimal
dispatching and the proposed risk-averse reinforcement learn-
ing are compared via the following two cases with the bid-
ding procedure simulated using the proposed DRMA-DDPG
method. In case 1, the DSO implements the market clearing
using the conventional optimal dispatching method introduced
in [32], and in case 2, the DSO clears the market by using
the proposed DRMA-DDPG algorithm. The penalty is set as
30% of the original value to avoid irrational bidding decisions
mentioned in the previous section. The market clearing results
of case 1 and 2 are shown in Fig. 8 and Fig. 9, respectively.
Regarding capacity allocation, from 1AM to 7AM, the
DSO with DRMA-DDPG method deploys less capacity from
VPP1 and more from VPP2 than that with the conventional
optimal dispatching method. This is because the large pene-
tration of wind generation in VPP1 results in potential penalty
for real-time imbalance. From 10AM to 3PM, the DSO with
DRMA-DDPG method deploys less capacity from VPP2 and
more from VPP3, VPP4 and VMG1 than that with optimal
dispatching method, for the sake of large penetration of PV
generation in VPP2. Such a conservative dispatching method
will increase the cost for purchasing the energy as a result
and, therefore, slightly decrease the average reward in some
time slots, for example, 2AM, 5AM, 2PM, etc. However, the
proposed method could effectively mitigate the huge penalty of
real-time imbalance in some time slots, and the corresponding
reward of DSO is increased, as shown at 3AM, 4AM, 10AM,
3PM, etc. The results indicate the advantages of the proposed
DRMA-DDPG method in the aspect of risk mitigation.
V. CONCLUSION
The optimal bi-level bidding and dispatching strategy in the
ADN with participation of DVAs is an emerging problem to be
investigated. In this paper, the proposed DRMA-DDPG algo-
rithm is employed as a risk-averse and fully distributed method
to simulate the dynamic bidding strategy optimization process,
and obtain the RNE solution. In the case studies, the advan-
tages of this proposed algorithm in terms of convergence speed
and risk aversion are demonstrated via benchmarking with sev-
eral existing RL algorithms. In addition, by simulating the
bidding procedure among DVAs in 24 hours, the signiﬁcance
and impacts of the penalty for misconducting the DA appoint-
ment and the total load demand on the bidding decision of
DVAs are found, and the parameters of market regulation
should therefore be carefully determined. Furthermore, at the
initial operation stage of new electricity market paradigms,
the risk-averse bidding strategy proposed in this paper will be
adopted by a large number of stakeholders, and the proposed
algorithm can be adopted to simulate the bidding procedure
of emerging markets and provide the theoretical and practical
supports for market operation.
