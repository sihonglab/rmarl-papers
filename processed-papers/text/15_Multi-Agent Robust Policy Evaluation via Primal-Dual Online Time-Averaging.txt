Time-averaging

Abstract
We investigate the distributed policy evaluation problem in multi-agent rein-
forcement learning process. Two cases are considered, where in the ﬁrst case,
a group of agents carry out the parallel computing to learn the value function
of a given joint policy and, for the second case, the sate space is partitioned
into many subspaces and each agent implements the distributed exploration
in each subspace by a given policy. These two cases are put into the cooper-
ative framework, where the mean squared projected Bellman error(MSPBE)
minimization problem for policy evaluation is constructed and then is refor-
mulated as a primal-dual saddle point problem. Compared with the existing
policy evaluation schemes, the proposed policy evaluation scheme can im-
prove the robustness performance of estimation from two aspects. First, the
primal-dual online time-averaging is considered and designed, potentially re-
ducing the eﬀects of external noises on the estimations. Second, the general
time-varying and jointly connected agent network is considered, which has
the potential to reduce the eﬀects of uncertain environment on the commu-
nications between agents. It is proved that the algorithm achieves sublinear
convergence by selecting appropriate gradient learning rates. Finally, numer-
ical experiments validate our algorithm.
Keywords:
Reinforcement learning, distributed policy evaluation,
distributed optimization, multi-agent systems

1. Introduction
Reinforcement learning aims to ﬁnd the best sequence of actions that will
generate the optimal return, which has achieved obvious breakthroughs in
many areas such as the Internet of Things (IoT) [1], smart grid [2], games [3],
and so on. Since some complex tasks require the collaborative cooperation of
multiple agents[4], multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has attracted
much more attention in recent years. In contrast to a single agent, each agent
in a multi-agent system needs to interact not only with the environment but
also with other agents to update the state of itself [5], which makes the task
of MARL more challenging.
In this paper, we consider two cases, where the ﬁrst case considers a group
of agents which carry out the parallel computing to learn the value function
of a given joint policy, and the second case partitions the state space into
many subspaces and each agent implements the distributed exploration in
each subspace by a given policy.
An example for the ﬁrst case is that a
group of robots cooperate to move an object as smoothly as possible. Each
robot may have diﬀerent actions and get diﬀerent reward values, but the
global state is common. The example for the second case is the distributed
exploring problem where multiple agents with distinct states and rewards
collect datasets collaboratively to evaluate the given policy.
The goal of
these cases is to maximize the sum of each agent’s local rewards. To achieve
this global goal, agents need to exchange local information collaboratively.
The problem of multi-agent reinforcement learning in this mode is inspired
by many real-life applications, such as sensor networks [6], smart grid [7],
energy management [8], and so on.
One direct way to solve the policy evaluation problem of collaborative
MARL is to utilize a central controller, which could collect the local re-
ward information of all agents and calculate the estimated policy value. In
this way, we can utilize the traditional single-agent reinforcement learning
scheme. However, the use of the central controller may face a lot of prob-
lems. For example, it is too expensive to maintain after the attack or failure
and cannot protect privacy. In some scenarios, some agents do not share local
information for security and privacy reasons. Therefore, another solution is
to utilize a fully distributed control framework in which the agent shares lo-
cal information with its neighbors through the communication network, and
the decision-making process is determined by the state of the agent itself and
the information received. It is like applying parallel ideas to deal with big

data problems [9, 10]. Motivated by the pioneering work of [11, 12, 13], we
mainly focus on the distributed policy evaluation problem of MARL.
The development of MARL can be traced back to Littman’s pioneering
work on the Markov game [14]. Most of the early works are based on the
tabular setting, which may face the curse of dimensionality. To solve this
problem, a group of agents is used to learn the approximate value function
of the centralized reward under a given policy, and a distributed gradient
temporal-diﬀerence (GTD) method is proposed in [15]. The work [16] stud-
ied two fully decentralized actor-critic algorithms to solve the problem of
linear function approximation in policy evaluation, which could tackle large-
scale MARL problems.
In [17], a variance-reduced method based on the
eligibility traces is presented. Diﬀerent from the gradient-based methods in
policy evaluation problems, a distributed alternating directions method of
multipliers (ADMM) algorithm is proposed in [18]. Some works use deep
neural networks to approximate the value functions [19]. By the primal-dual
reformulation of policy evaluation in reinforcement learning, some central-
ized algorithms are investigated, such as GTD2 [20], PDBG and SVAG [13].
The work [11] uses Fenchel duality to transform the minimized mean square
Bellman error (MSPBE) into a kind of distributed saddle-point problem. By
using the proposed double averaging algorithm, a fully distributed MARL
setting is realized.
Most of the existing works are feasible under the framework of undirected
and ﬁxed topologies [11, 17, 18]. Due to the actual privacy consideration, an
agent may broadcast its local information to another agent, but the receiver
may be unwilling to transmit the local information back to the previous
agent. Moreover, in the uncertain environment, the communication network
may be time-varying. Therefore, to tackle this problem, we mainly focus
on a sequence of time-varying and jointly connected digraphs in this paper.
Motivated by the Laplacian averaging [21], we combine the MARL with
primal-dual running-time averaging in the process of policy evaluation.
The main contributions are twofold. First, we establish a distributed pol-
icy evaluation algorithm with primal-dual online time-averaging. The online
time-averaging scheme has the potential to reduce the eﬀects of ubiquitous
noises on policy estimation. Therefore, the proposed scheme is more suitable
for the online learning process. Second, considering the uncertainty and ef-
fects of the external environment, the agent network may be time-varying or
even not be connected in some instants. The general communication network
with time-varying and jointly connected topology is considered in this work.

Motivated by the running time-average analyses [21], we shed some light on

5. Conclusions
In this paper, we studied the distributed policy evaluation problem for
multi-agent network under the time-varying and jointly connected topologies.
Using Fencel dual theory, the policy evaluation problem was converted into a
distributed primal-dual optimization problem. The distributed primal-dual
online averaging scheme was proposed and the convergence rates under the
constant learning rate and the time-varying learning rate were derived and
analyzed. The numerical experiments veriﬁed the convergence performance.
