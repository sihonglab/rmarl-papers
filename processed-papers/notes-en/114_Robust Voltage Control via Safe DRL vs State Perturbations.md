# 114. Robust Voltage Control for Active Distribution Networks via Safe Deep Reinforcement Learning Against State Perturbations

## Taxonomy
- **Robustness / perturbation type targeted**: State/observation perturbation (noisy/contaminated measurements of voltage and power-flow profile in distribution grids, caused by instrument errors and transmission noise), together with safety constraints (voltage limits)
- **Method paradigm**: Safe deep RL / constrained MDP (CMDP) with Lagrange multipliers; multi-agent actor-critic (MADDPG, CTDE); adversarial state attacker (C-MRC); robust regulation loss (worst-case / minimax over perturbed states)
- **Keywords**: Active distribution network, robust voltage control, state perturbation, model-free, safe deep reinforcement learning, MADDPG, CMDP

## TL;DR
The paper proposes RS-MADDPG, a robust safe multi-agent DRL method for voltage regulation of active distribution networks that formulates the problem as a constrained Markov decision process (CMDP) solved by a safety-augmented MADDPG and adds a novel "robust regulation loss" — driven by a C-MRC state adversary — to maintain voltage safety and minimize line losses under varying state perturbations.

## Problem & Motivation
With rising penetration of renewable distributed energy resources (DERs), especially photovoltaics (PVs), active distribution networks (ADNs) suffer voltage deviation and power-quality issues. Traditional voltage control faces a trade-off between efficiency and effectiveness: model-based optimization depends on a precise physical model (hard to obtain in real grids and computationally heavy with many DERs), while existing DRL/MARL-based methods do not always keep voltages within safe limits and require perturbation-free observations to perform well. In practice, observed states are inevitably contaminated by measurement errors and transmission noise (state perturbations), which degrade controller reliability and robustness. The paper targets robust, safe, model-free voltage control under such state perturbations.

## Robustness Setting
- **Threat model / uncertainty set**: State perturbations applied to the observed grid states (voltages, active/reactive power of PVs and loads). Perturbations are modeled as zero-mean truncated Gaussian distributions δ ~ N_trunc(0, σ), guaranteeing a compact noise set and avoiding unrealistic anomalous perturbations; σ dominates the disturbance magnitude. During testing, perturbations are applied multiplicatively to state values (s_o = s(1 + δ)). A state-of-the-art adversary, the Combined Maximum Reward and Cost (C-MRC) attacker, treats the state as a variable and uses gradient ascent to maximize the reward and cost functions, stealthily inducing the controller into safety-violating policies, to generate worst-case perturbed states for training.
- **Setting**: Cooperative multi-agent (each PV is an independent agent; cooperative reward); centralized-training-decentralized-execution (CTDE); model-free / online (off-policy MADDPG).

## Method
- Formulates voltage control as a multi-agent CMDP / safe Markov game: maximize expected discounted return (negative voltage deviation, reactive-power and curtailment costs) subject to a cost constraint penalizing overvoltage/voltage violations (a binary "hard restriction" cost on the fraction of violating nodes).
- Develops S-MADDPG: a safety-augmented MADDPG (CTDE actor-critic). It introduces an independent cost-value network (cost critic) trained with TD loss, converts the constrained objective into an unconstrained max-min problem via Lagrange multipliers (updated by ReLU-gated gradient descent to enforce non-negativity), and uses parameter sharing across agents for efficiency.
- Introduces the robust regulation loss: starting from a worst-case (min over perturbations) reformulation of the actor objective and an upper bound on the expected-return loss under the optimal perturbation, it adds an l2-normed regularization term that penalizes the deviation D(π(s), π(s̃)) between policies under natural and perturbed states.
- Uses the C-MRC attacker (Algorithm 2) to obtain optimal perturbed states that maximize the regularization term; combining S-MADDPG with the robust regulation loss yields RS-MADDPG (Algorithm 1), trained with a progressive (ascent-schedule) perturbation scale.

## Theoretical Contributions
None / mostly empirical. The paper invokes (citing prior work) an upper bound on the expected-return loss under worst-case state perturbations (Eq. 18) to motivate the robust regulation loss, but provides no new convergence or sample-complexity guarantees.

## Experiments
- **Environment/Benchmark**: Mutated IEEE 33-bus and 141-bus distribution test cases (plain versions from MATPOWER), with 6 and 22 PV generator/inverter pairs added, respectively. Implemented with Pandapower and PyTorch. Real-world Portuguese load data and Elia-group PV generation data (3-year span, 3-minute sampling) with added Gaussian noise.
- **Baselines**: Vanilla MADDPG, S-MADDPG (ablation), a conventional model-based method (second-order cone programming / SOCP solved with GUROBI), and other MARL frameworks adapted into the proposed CMDP + robust regulation loss framework: RS-MATD3 (from MATD3), RS-MAPPO (from MAPPO), and a classical Lyapunov-based SDRL method.
- **Evaluation metrics**: Power Loss (PL, mean energy loss over buses, in MW) and Controllable Rate (CR, average fraction of time steps where all buses stay within voltage limits), reported as mean ± std over five seeds; training reward/cost convergence curves. Tested under perturbation scales σ ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5} (and {1.0, 2.0, 3.0} for cross-method comparison).

## Key Results
- Under no perturbation, the model-based SOCP method dominates on both PL and CR; among model-free methods, S-MADDPG attains high voltage safety (CR) close to the model-based approach, while vanilla MADDPG has lower line loss but weaker voltage safety.
- Under state perturbations, RS-MADDPG surpasses other benchmarks on most cases: at σ = 3.0 its performance is nearly equivalent to vanilla MADDPG in the unperturbed natural state, and across σ ∈ {0.5…2.5} it maintains CR ≥ 93% (33-bus) and ≥ 99% (141-bus) even at the most severe σ = 2.5.
- The CMDP formulation + robust regulation loss generalize to other MARL algorithms (RS-MATD3, RS-MAPPO), significantly improving voltage stability across scenarios; RS-MADDPG gives the most elite controllable-ratio performance and outperforms the Lyapunov-based SDRL under intensifying perturbations.

## Limitations & Future Work
- The model-based SOCP approach still gives better PL when observations are close to true states; RS-MADDPG mainly regains line-loss superiority only at larger perturbations (σ > 2), and in the 141-bus case its PL is slightly higher than its vanilla benchmark.
- Robust regulation loss optimizes for worst-case scenarios, so it is hard to outperform under ideal, perturbation-free test conditions.
- Future work: extend RS-MADDPG to three-phase unbalanced distribution networks; study "worst-case" robustness for other uncertainty aspects (topology, network parameters, PV-generation and load uncertainties) toward a robust synthesis resolving most distribution-network uncertainty problems.

## Relevance to Survey
This is an applied robust-MARL paper situated on the "state/observation perturbation" robustness line combined with the "safety-constrained (CMDP)" line, applied to power-system voltage control. It explicitly adopts the adversarial state-perturbation paradigm (worst-case policy deviation, an upper bound on return loss following Zhang et al. on robust DRL against state-observation perturbations, and a C-MRC safe-RL adversary), inside a cooperative CTDE MADDPG framework. It connects robust MARL with safe RL and adversarial state attacks, and is a domain-specific instantiation of robust regularization for MARL policies under noisy observations.

## Related Work (verbatim excerpts from the paper)
> _[Section I, Introduction — DRL/MARL and safe DRL for voltage control]_

"With the growing adoption of artificial intelligence technologies, advanced control algorithms, particularly those based on deep reinforcement learning (DRL), have been applied to a wide range of power system tasks [22][25], partially overcoming the limitations of traditional voltage control methods. DRL is a fully data-driven approach uses operational data interacting with the environment to pre-train decision models, which can achieve real-time on-the-fly management for fast-responding voltage regulation devices, such as PV inverters [26][28], static var compensators (SVCs) [29][30], etc. In addition, when the PV penetration in ADN is extremely high, the multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) methods adopt the divide-and-conquer philosophy, and can circumvent the dimensional explosion problem caused by centralized management and considerably reduce the computational burden of the controller. In order to enhance the collaboration of agents, MARL-based voltage control methods tend to utilize the algorithms with centralized training and decentralized execution (CTDE) framework, such as multi-agent deep Q network (MADQN) [31], multi-agent deep deterministic policy gradient (MADDPG) [32][35] and multi-agent soft actor-critic (MASAC) [36][37], to achieve a remarkable trade-off between communication cost and globally optimal decision-making. In general, DRL-based voltage control methods involve voltage constraints and energy loss as the objective function for optimization. However, in pursuing overall optimum, these methods do not always ensure that voltages remain within safe operating limits [38], which may pose risks to the safe operation of ADNs. To address this problem, safe deep reinforcement learning (SDRL) has been widely explored. Reference [39] formulates the optimal operation problem of distribution networks as a constrained Markov decision process (CMDP), utilizing the constrained policy optimization (CPO) algorithm to achieve minimal power cost while keeping voltages under regulation by coordinating conventional volt-var control (VVC) devices. An online multi-agent constrained soft actor-critic (MACSAC) with decentralized control framework is developed to fulfill VVC against physical model uncertainty [40], whereas in [41], a model-free constrained soft actor-critic (CSAC) algorithm is proposed for VVC, which formulates the voltage violations as constraints instead of rewards. Reference [42] presents a DRL algorithm associated with a safety module in the medium resolution of three stages VVC to schedule PV inventers and energy storage systems (ESSs), which eliminates voltage deviations throughout the online training phase. However, the aforementioned methods have slightly lower ceilings on voltage stability and power loss attenuation comparing to the conventional model-based ones, and meanwhile they require perturbation-free observational states to achieve decent performance."

> _[Section I, Introduction — uncertainties, state perturbations, and robust optimization]_

"Uncertainty problems are widely pervasive throughout power systems, such as disturbances in measured states [43][44], nebulous grid parameters and topologies [45][47], and uncertainties of PVs and loads [48][50]. Such indeterminacy significantly degrades the reliability and robustness of voltage control, with state disturbances being the most prominent. The state perturbation generally refers to misperception of present voltage or power flow profile across the whole ADN, typically caused by instrument errors [51], which significantly affects the decision-making ability of voltage controllers to coordinate active and reactive devices. Addressing the impact of these uncertainties on distribution network optimization strategies, enhancing algorithm robustness is increasingly becoming a focal issue in the field of voltage control in distribution networks recently [52]. In [53], a robust optimization method is also introduced to obtain Pareto solutions under uncertainties, and a multi-objective adaptive VVC is proposed to address voltage issues in ADNs with high PV penetration. Reference [54] introduces a two-stage approach with distributed optimization for classical voltage control devices and robust optimization for PV inverters, employing a non-centralized VVC algorithm. A universal distributionally robust safety filter for VVC is developed to use robust optimization with chance constraints to ensure near-optimal solutions and constraint satisfaction [55]. Considering bad data in measurement, reference [56] proposes a robust VVC approach by combining revised ADMM and local feedback control. Nevertheless, the aforementioned methods primarily leverages the principles of robust optimization. Although robust optimization is widely applied to address robust voltage control issues in distribution networks considering uncertainties, it still faces the issue of overly conservative optimization strategies due to its focus on the \"worst-case\" scenario. This highlights a new direction for subsequent research. Among various uncertainties, this paper primarily considers the impact of state perturbations on distribution network voltage optimization."

> _[Section III.C, Robust Regulation Loss — bound on return loss under state perturbations]_

"Notwithstanding deriving the optimal deterministic policy π* under the optimal perturbation ν* may present inherent difficulties, it is still possible to establish an upper bound on the expected return loss when facing the worst conditions, as compared to scenarios where perturbations are absent [63]"

> _[Section III, Proposed Voltage Control Method — C-MRC state adversary]_

"Regarding the problem of solving for the optimal perturbation, a state-of-the-art state adversary known as the combined maximum reward and cost (C-MRC) attacker [64] is employed. This adversary treats the state as a variable and leverages gradient ascent to maximize the reward and cost functions, stealthily enticing the controller to generate policies that violate safety constraints. The C-MRC attacker has demonstrated excellent performance in safe reinforcement learning, making it an ideal choice for generating perturbated states which effectively maximize the regularization term."
