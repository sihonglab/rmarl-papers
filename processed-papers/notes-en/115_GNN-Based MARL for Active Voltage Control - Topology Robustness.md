# 115. Graph Neural Network-Based Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Active Voltage Control: Performance and Topology Robustness

## Metadata
- **Title**: Graph Neural Network-Based Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Active Voltage Control: Performance and Topology Robustness
- **Authors**: Chijioke Eze, Chetan Prakash, Antonello Monti
- **Affiliation**: Institute for Automation of Complex Power Systems, RWTH Aachen University; Faculty of Computer Science, RWTH Aachen University; Fraunhofer FIT, Sankt Augustin, Germany
- **Venue**: Preprint submitted to Energy and AI, 2025 (not peer reviewed)
- **Link/arXiv**: https://ssrn.com/abstract=5960743

## Taxonomy
- **Robustness / perturbation type targeted**: Topology robustness (radial vs. looped/meshed network topologies); robustness to network-structure changes; observation/sensor measurement noise (Gaussian); scalability across network sizes. Note: this is application-domain robustness (power grid topology) rather than adversarial / worst-case robust MARL.
- **Method paradigm**: Topology-aware MARL with Graph Neural Network policy networks (GCN / GAT / GGCN), value decomposition via centralized critic, CTDE, MADDPG-based deterministic policy gradient
- **Keywords**: GNN, MARL, Distributed Voltage Control, Active Voltage Control, Power Systems, topology robustness, CTDE

## TL;DR
The paper develops a unified GNN-MARL framework (under CTDE) that systematically compares spectral (GCN), attention (GAT), and memory-based (GGCN) GNN policy networks for active voltage control on power distribution networks, and shows that topology-aware GNN policies outperform topology-unaware baselines, scale to large networks, and—notably—maintain near-identical performance on radial and looped (meshed) topologies, demonstrating strong topology robustness.

## Problem & Motivation
Active Voltage Control (AVC) in power distribution networks (PDNs) with high PV penetration must dynamically counter voltage fluctuations. Traditional OPF-based and droop-based methods require detailed/continuously-updated system models or manual tuning and scale poorly. Single-agent DRL controllers face a single point of failure and degrade as the network grows. MARL is a promising distributed alternative, but most prior MARL-based AVC work (a) uses GCN-only policies evaluated on small/medium systems, (b) benchmarks GNN policies against much smaller vector-based baselines (unfair comparison), and (c) focuses exclusively on radial topologies. The paper fills these gaps with a systematic, size-matched comparison of GNN families, the first MARL evaluation on looped networks, and an analysis of edge-feature value.

## Robustness Setting
- **Threat model / uncertainty set**: No adversary. Robustness here is to (i) network topology changes—radial vs. looped/meshed configurations created by re-introducing switchable lines—and (ii) sensor measurement noise modeled as an isotropic Gaussian on observations, Ω(o_{t+1} | s_{t+1}, a_t) = s_{t+1} + N(0, Σ). Noise-perturbed load/PV profiles are used during training "to improve the robustness of the learned policy." Operating uncertainty also includes load/PV variability and varying network scales (IEEE-33/141/322).
- **Setting**: cooperative (each PV inverter is an agent, shared global reward); CTDE (centralized critic, decentralized GNN actors); online deep RL (MADDPG with experience replay). Modeled as a Dec-POMDP ⟨I, S, A, O, T, r, Ω, ρ, γ⟩.

## Method
- Formulate PV-inverter-based AVC as a Dec-POMDP; each PV is an agent that outputs a continuous reactive-power action a_i ∈ [−c, c] mapping to q^PV_i = a_i·sqrt((s^max_i)² − (p^PV_i)²); global reward r balances a voltage barrier penalty l_v(v_i) against a reactive-power-effort term α·l_q(q^PV).
- Build a unified GNN-MARL framework: zone-based local observations are converted into graph-structured data (node features [v_j, p^L_j, q^L_j, p^PV_j], edge_index) and processed by GNN policy networks; a shared centralized MLP critic aggregates joint observations/actions during training.
- Implement three GNN policy families of comparable parameter size to a vector/RNN baseline: GCN (spectral normalized propagation), GAT (learned attention over neighbors), and GGCN (recurrent gated message passing via GRU); evaluate three voltage barrier functions (L1, L2, Bowl).
- Train with MADDPG: experience replay, deterministic policy-gradient actor updates, TD critic updates, soft target updates θ′ ← τθ + (1−τ)θ′ (Algorithm 1). Safety mechanisms: (i) barrier-shaped reward, (ii) action clipping to inverter capacity, (iii) zero-reactive fallback for infeasible power-flow states.
- Evaluate topology robustness by extending radial IEEE systems into looped/meshed variants (re-introducing switchable lines, verifying power-flow convergence), and analyze whether adding explicit edge features (GATe) helps.

## Theoretical Contributions
None / mostly empirical. The paper offers design rationale (permutation-equivariance of GNN encoders explaining cross-topology transfer; over-smoothing on loops for GCN; gating/attention curbing signal recirculation) and computational-complexity characterizations (GCN O(|E|), GAT O(|E|·H), GGCN O(T·|E|·D²) time / O(T·|V|·D) space), but no convergence, sample-complexity, or certified-robustness proofs.

## Experiments
- **Environment/Benchmark**: IEEE-33 (small), IEEE-141 (medium), IEEE-322 (large) distribution test systems, plus looped/meshed variants; Portuguese electricity consumption data (232 customer load profiles, 3-minute resolution); simulated with Pandapower (OPF in MATLAB). Three voltage barrier functions: L1, L2, Bowl.
- **Baselines**: Topology-unaware MARL with an RNN (GRU) policy trained via MADDPG; traditional optimization-based OPF (taken as the theoretical upper bound). Also compares standard GAT vs. GATe (GAT with edge features).
- **Evaluation metrics**: Controllable Rate (CR, ↑), Power Loss (PL, ↓ in MW), Voltage Out of Control (VOC, ↓). Results averaged over 6 random seeds.

## Key Results
- GNN policies match baselines on small networks but show clear advantages on medium/large networks; best per-size configs: GCN-BL (IEEE-33), GGCN-BL (IEEE-141), GAT-BL (IEEE-322). On IEEE-141, GGCN-BL improves VOC/CR/PL by ~46.58%/21.75%/53.00% over the best RNN baseline; on IEEE-322, GAT-BL improves by ~50.10%/21.33%/23.74%.
- Topology robustness: GNN-MARL maintains near-identical performance on radial and looped networks (CR within ~5% variance), whereas RNN baselines drop 20–30% in CR across topologies; GGCN-L2 best on the looped case (VOC 1.07%, CR 92.88%, PL 0.668 MW). This is reported as the first MARL/RL AVC study on looped (meshed) networks.
- Edge features (GATe) help only on the voltage-sensitive IEEE-141; on IEEE-33, IEEE-322, and IEEE-33-loop standard GAT outperforms GATe—node connectivity (adjacency) alone is generally sufficient.
- Vs. OPF (theoretical upper bound): GNN-MARL closely tracks OPF on small nets, lags on medium, and exceeds OPF on VOC for the largest network (IEEE-322), while consistently yielding the lowest power loss across all sizes; GAT is the overall best policy (1st or 2nd in every scenario).

## Limitations & Future Work
- No universally optimal voltage barrier function exists; the best barrier depends on network and policy architecture (open challenge).
- GGCN performance degrades on larger networks (higher computational/space complexity); GCN suffers over-smoothing on looped topologies.
- Preprint, not peer reviewed; robustness is empirical (no certified/worst-case guarantees) and limited to simulated IEEE systems.
- Future work: spatio-temporal GNNs for evolving topologies, richer topological abstractions (e.g., hypergraphs), real-world hardware-in-the-loop testing, probabilistic uncertainty quantification, and multi-objective optimization over voltage quality, economics, and stability.

## Relevance to Survey
This is an application-domain (power-grid) MARL paper whose "robustness" contribution is topology robustness—stable performance of cooperative CTDE MARL policies under structural changes (radial → looped/meshed) and observation noise—rather than adversarial or distributionally robust MARL. It connects the survey's themes on (i) robustness to environment-structure / distribution shift, (ii) communication/topology-aware MARL architectures (GNN message passing), and (iii) robustness to input/observation noise. It is a useful example of how inductive biases (permutation-equivariant graph encoders) confer empirical robustness, complementing the worst-case / minimax robust-MARL lines that dominate the survey.

"Due to the limitations of traditional AVC techniques and the success of single-agent RL in various control problems [? ? ], researchers in the power systems community have started applying DRL to AVC problem [? ? ]. Unlike classical methods that depend on predefined models and extensive manual tuning, DRL-based approaches learn control policies directly from their interactions with the system. This allows them to naturally cope with nonlinear dynamics, uncertainty, and time-varying operating conditions without the need for explicit modeling. Moreover, once trained, DRL controllers are capable of making fast real-time decisions and can more readily adapt to changes in grid conditions caused by renewable output variability or load fluctuations than conventional techniques. However, as the network grows in size and complexity, the effectiveness and scalability of single-agent DRL approaches deteriorate because a single controller must process high-dimensional system states and simultaneously manage multiple controllable devices. In addition, by being centralized, these DRL-based AVC architectures are vulnerable to a single point of failure."

"To overcome the drawbacks of traditional AVC and single-agent DRL, and to meet the demand for fast response and effective coordination, deep MARL, a distributed and data-driven framework for multi-agent control problems [? ? ], is gaining a growing interest as a promising solution for AVC [? ? ? ]. Existing MARL-based AVC studies can be broadly grouped into two categories: region-based methods [? ? ? ] and PV-based methods [? ? ]. In region-based schemes, the PDN is partitioned into several regions according to its tree-graph structure, and each region is assigned a dedicated agent. MARL algorithms are then applied to these agents to solve the AVC problem. For instance, Cao et al. [? ] apply MADDPG [? ] to the regional agents to regulate the reactive power of PV inverters within their respective regions. Similarly, Sun and Qiu [? ] integrate an OPF-based strategy with MADDPG to manage regional reactive power, achieving enhanced control performance. In [? ], spectral clustering is used to define the control relationships among regional agents, and the resulting AVC task is addressed using MATD3 [? ], substantially reducing communication requirements. While these region-based strategies perform well on small-scale PDNs, their effectiveness degrades in larger networks due to heterogeneous PV counts and network topologies [? ]. This has led to the rise of PV-based approaches, which avoid inconsistent regional structures by treating each PV inverter as an individual agent. Interest in this formulation for AVC is rapidly increasing. A representative example is the work of Wang et al. [? ], where PV agents within the same region share local observations, demonstrating the strong potential of PV-based MARL for AVC in large-scale benchmark networks."

> _[Section 1.1, Background and Related Work]_

"Further, it has been observed that most most of the existing DRL and MARL approaches are designed for data that lie in Euclidean spaces, where deep neural networks (DNNs) are naturally formulated [? ]. In contrast, the information relevant to AVC such as bus (node) voltage magnitudes and network topology is intrinsically graph-structured and non-Euclidean. This type of data involves unordered nodes, variable graph sizes, and heterogeneous neighborhood relationships that standard DNNs are not well-suited to model. Conventional DNN architectures process node features as fixed, ordered input vectors and therefore fail to capture the relational and topological dependencies that are essential characteristics of power grid networks [? ]. To leverage the rich structural information of power grids, recent work has begun integrating GNNs into DRL [? ? ? ] and MARL [? ? ? ] frameworks for AVC, using them as policy architectures and reporting gains in control performance and robustness to input noise. In addition, [? ] introduces an inverter-based AVC scheme employing a GCN as the critic network while retaining a conventional DNN for the policy network, also achieving improved control performance. However, a key limitation of that method is the substantial power loss observed even on a moderately sized system (IEEE-141), which is likely to become more severe for larger networks, making the approach operationally costly from the operator's standpoint."
