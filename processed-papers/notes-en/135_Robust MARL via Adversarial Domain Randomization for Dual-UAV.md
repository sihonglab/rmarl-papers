# 135. Robust Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Method Based on Adversarial Domain Randomization for Real-World Dual-UAV Cooperation

## Metadata
- **Title**: Robust Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Method Based on Adversarial Domain Randomization for Real-World Dual-UAV Cooperation
- **Authors**: Shutong Chen, Guanjun Liu, Ziyuan Zhou, Kaiwen Zhang, Jiacun Wang
- **Affiliation**: Department of Computer Science, Tongji University, Shanghai, China; Department of Computer Science and Software Engineering, Monmouth University, West Long Branch, NJ, USA
- **Venue**: IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Vehicles, Vol. 9, No. 1, January 2024
- **Link/arXiv**: DOI: 10.1109/TIV.2023.3307134

## Taxonomy
- **Robustness / perturbation type targeted**: Sim-to-real gap / environment (model) uncertainty modeled via domain randomization parameters (e.g., main engine strength, GPS position noise, and other physical-property variations); worst-case over randomization parameters.
- **Method paradigm**: Adversarial training with a "nature player" (adversarial environment generator), adversarial stationary Markov game, minimax (max over agent policy / min over randomization parameter), game-theoretic Nash equilibrium, R-MADDPG actor-critic, prioritized experience replay.
- **Keywords**: Multi-agent reinforcement learning, sim2real transfer, adversarial domain randomization, prioritized experience replay, dual-UAV cooperative transportation

## TL;DR
The paper proposes an adversarial domain randomization (ADR) method that formalizes domain-randomized MARL as an adversarial stationary Markov game and uses a "nature player" to generate worst-case training environments, plus a prioritized experience replay scheme (PERDRE), enabling a stable, transferable dual-UAV cooperative transportation policy trained in simulation and deployed to real UAVs.

## Problem & Motivation
MARL is a promising way to control closely cooperating multi-UAV systems (e.g., two UAVs transporting a payload), but it relies on extensive trial-and-error exploration, which is hard to gather in the real world. Policies are therefore trained in simulation and transferred to reality, but a sim-to-real gap (noise, sensor errors, dynamic changes) prevents reliable transfer. Domain randomization is an effective bridge, yet the traditional uniform-sampling version has two shortcomings: (1) the trained policy is unstable, with high-variance performance across environments generated by different degrees of randomization parameters, and (2) training converges slowly and is costly. The paper addresses both: it makes the policy more robust to challenging randomized situations and accelerates convergence.

## Robustness Setting
- **Threat model / uncertainty set**: Domain randomization parameters u drawn from a bounded space U parameterize the transition and reward functions; their effect on reward and transition is deterministic, so r^i_s and P_s belong to compact sets. Instead of uniform selection, a "nature player" policy μ: S → U adversarially selects worst-case randomization parameters, and the agent maximizes expected reward under this worst case (max over π, min over u). Six domain randomization parameters with predefined ranges are used in the dual-UAV task (and main engine strength in LunarLander-v2).
- **Setting**: cooperative (agents share a team reward); CTDE (centralized training and decentralized execution, with LSTM to infer the randomization-parameter distribution); online training in simulation followed by sim2real transfer (distributed hierarchical deployment).

## Method
- Defines a nonstationary Markov game (from prior work, with a domain-randomization parameter set U) and then introduces the "nature player" μ to convert it into an adversarial stationary Markov game with tuple ⟨N, S, {A^i}, {O^i}, O_μ, {R^i}, U, T, γ⟩; the worst-case Bellman equation is V^i_*(s) = max_{π^i} min_{u∈U} Σ_a Π_j π^j(a^j|s)[r^i(s,a,u) + γ Σ_{s'} P(s'|s,a,u) V^i_*(s')], with the inner min realized by a deterministic policy μ over U.
- Builds the training algorithm on R-MADDPG (a recurrent extension of MADDPG with LSTM cell states for actor/critic), augmenting its policy gradient (Eq. 11) and critic loss (Eq. 12) to condition the value function on the randomization parameter u; the nature player (Adversarial Environment Generator) is an encoder-decoder network with parameter δ.
- Trains the nature player with loss = α0·r + β0·kl − ω0·mse, where r is the average agent reward in the generated environment, kl is the KL divergence between the latent distribution of the generated u and the normal distribution of u, and mse is the mean squared error between observed o_μ and generated u (ensuring randomness); α0, β0, ω0 ∈ [0,1] balance how adversarial the generated environment is.
- Introduces PERDRE (prioritized experience replay for domain randomization experiences): uses critic loss to measure each experience's influence, partitions experiences into K subsets by summed normalized randomization parameters, tracks per-subset replay counts, and computes a sampling probability from an importance weight Iw = Rank(L) + Rank'(C) to sample critical and under-sampled experiences, speeding convergence without hurting performance.
- Deploys via distributed hierarchical sim2real transfer: a three-layer software stack (Data Perception, Action Decision, Low-level Control) where the ADR-trained Action Decision policy is transferred unchanged and only perception modules are swapped for real-world-trained ones.

## Theoretical Contributions
- **Property 1**: For finite state space S, randomization space U, and action space A, a nonstationary Markov game has at least one nonstationary Markov perfect Nash equilibrium (invoking Proposition 2.2 of [45] on robust Markov games with reward and transition perturbations).
- **Property 2**: The adversarial stationary Markov game (with nature player) has the same value-function form as a standard Markov game, so at least one perfect Nash equilibrium (μ*, π*) exists (via the existence of Nash equilibrium for any Markov game, [39]).
- Derivation of the Bellman equation for the value function in nonstationary Markov games (Appendix A).
- Time-complexity analysis: PERDRE (Algorithm 2) is O(d); the full Algorithm 1 over N agents and Z iterations is O(Z(D1 + D2 + D3 + N(D1 + d + D4))).

## Experiments
- **Environment/Benchmark**: (1) LunarLander-v2 (OpenAI Gym) with main engine strength (MES) as the randomization factor; (2) Dual-UAV cooperative transportation in an AirSim/Unreal Engine simulation (six domain randomization parameters), plus real-world dual-UAV flight trials (Sim2Real).
- **Baselines**: In LunarLander-v2: ADRDQN (ours) vs. UDRDQN (uniform/traditional domain randomization) vs. DQN (fixed environment, no randomization). In dual-UAV: ADRMA (ours) vs. UDRMA (traditional domain randomization) vs. R-MADDPG (fixed environment, no randomization). PERDRE compared against traditional random experience replay (RER).
- **Evaluation metrics**: Mean reward (LunarLander, success at reward > 200); task completion rate and completion timesteps/time (dual-UAV) under varying single-parameter noise (GPS position) and under fully uniform domain randomization; training reward convergence (episodes to converge).

## Key Results
- LunarLander-v2: when MES is 12–13 all three policies succeed; as MES decreases, DQN fails to adapt, UDRDQN struggles at smaller MES, while ADRDQN remains stable in challenging environments and still adapts to normal ones.
- Dual-UAV (single-parameter GPS noise): ADRMA keeps a task success rate over 75% across all noise levels; UDRMA holds around 65% at moderate noise but degrades as noise grows; R-MADDPG cannot adapt to large noise.
- Dual-UAV (fully uniform randomization): ADRMA reaches about 80% test success rate and is relatively stable, UDRMA about 73% with higher variability, and R-MADDPG only about 45% with high variability; ADRMA and UDRMA also improve completion timesteps.
- PERDRE converges around 6000 episodes vs. about 10000 for RER (out of 20000; ~45 h total), speeding convergence without reducing performance. In real-world Sim2Real trials, R-MADDPG struggles to adapt; ADRMA and UDRMA both perform well in Cases I–II, while ADRMA is more stable than UDRMA in Cases III–IV (though sometimes needing more time).

## Limitations & Future Work
- Serial interaction with the simulation engine is slow (20000 episodes ≈ 45 hours), and Bayesian-optimization-style real-experience methods are deemed infeasible for high-risk multi-UAV tasks.
- The single-dimension robustness analysis is argued to generalize to high-dimensional randomization, but the paper notes it is hard to intuitively define which high-dimensional environments are more challenging.
- In some real-world cases (III, IV) the more robust ADRMA policy requires more time to complete the task.
- Future work: combine ADR and PERDRE with different RL algorithms for more practical tasks, and explore further improvements to the domain randomization method.

## Relevance to Survey
A robust-MARL-for-sim2real work that directly adopts the "nature player" adversarial modeling paradigm from robust Markov games (explicitly building on Zhang et al.'s robust MARL with model uncertainty [45]) and recasts domain randomization as an adversarial stationary Markov game with worst-case (minimax) optimization over randomization parameters. It connects the environment/model-uncertainty robustness line and the adversarial-training/game-theoretic-equilibrium method line to a concrete real-world multi-UAV deployment, illustrating how robust MARL meets sim-to-real transfer.

## Related Work (verbatim excerpts from the paper)

> _[Section II, Related Work — A. MARL for UAV Control]_

"In the field of intelligent transportation, the problem of collaborative goal optimization of multiple agents is important and challenging [14]. Compared with a single UAV system, a multi-UAV system shows superior performance in terms of time efficiency, cost-effectiveness, collaborative action, complementary information, fault tolerance, and flexibility [15]. MARL has been used to train their control policies which enable them to accomplish a cooperative task [16]."

"MARL-based approaches for controlling clusters of multi-UAV in various tasks are investigated [17], [18], [19], [20]. In [9], [21], [22], [23], [24], [25], MARL is introduced to train control policies of swarms of UAVs to perform specific tasks such as environmental monitoring. These approaches include distributed reinforcement learning and communication reinforcement learning. A simulation scenario for UAVs transportation in a city and an improved Multi-Actor-Attention-Critic (MAAC) method to train the control policy for multi-UAV cooperative transportation in a city are discussed in [26] and [27]. The study in [28] shows the advantages of hierarchical optimization for dealing with complex tasks in multi-UAV control systems."

"It is worth noting that the above studies are conducted in various simulation environments and not experimentally validated in the real world. Bridging the gap between simulation and real-world environments to apply trained policies on real-world UAV control systems remains a significant challenge."

> _[Section II, Related Work — B. Domain Randomization]_

"Recently, a series of sim2real techniques [12] are proposed to bridge the gap between simulation and real-world environments. For example, Tobin et al. [13] propose a domain randomization method for the first time such that the deep neural network trained with the simulated images can be transferred to the real world. In the field of deep reinforcement learning, some studies also employ the domain randomization method to transfer policies trained in simulation environments to the real world. By employing it, Andrychowicz et al. [29] simulate changes in the physical properties of the system within a simulation environment and transfer the trained policy to a physical robot, achieving dexterous manipulation in robotics. The study in [30] trains an MARL policy in a domain randomization experimental environment and transfers it to the real-world for controlling multiple autonomous vehicles."

"The traditional domain randomization method often employs a uniform selection of domain randomization parameters within predefined ranges to generate a training environment. However, this method often results in an unstable reinforcement learning policy, characterized by high-variance performance when applied to environments generated by different degrees of domain randomization parameters [31]. In particular, it has to struggle to deal with complex situations. An active domain randomization method [31] introduces a discriminator that evaluates whether the experience of the current episode is from a reference or a randomization environment and selects the value of domain randomization parameters to generate the environment during the next training. Raparthy et al. [32] further improves this active method by using meta-learning. However, both of them rely on a reference environment that is based on prior knowledge and thus perform poorly in environments with multiple randomization parameters. The method in [33] optimizes the selection process of domain randomization environments based on Bayesian optimization, which collects a sufficient number of real experiences in a real-world environment. This method is more suitable for the controlled tasks such as mechanical arm control but is not too feasible for high-risk tasks such as multi-UAV cooperation."

> _[Section II, Related Work — C. Sample-Efficient Reinforcement Learning]_

"For reinforcement learning, the experience replay method is commonly utilized to enhance the efficiency of experience usage [34]. Agents can learn more effectively from specific experiences [35]. The prioritized experience replay method employs a biased importance sampling instead of a uniform sampling. Specifically, a temporal difference error is used to estimate the importance of an experience, and this method is validated through experiments conducted with the Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm in the Atari environment. Furthermore, Prioritized Sequence Experience Replay (PSER) [36] involves information from state transitions when estimating the importance of a temporal difference error. Emphasizing Recent Experience (ERE) [36] gives higher weights to recent experiences and shows better performance in continuous action space. Likelihood-Free Importance Weighting (LFIW) [37] uses the likelihood ratio method to estimate the importance of an experience."

"It is worth noting that these methods attempt to enhance the algorithm performance in general environments but can not directly be applied to a reinforcement learning training in an environment with domain randomization parameters."

> _[Section III, Preliminaries — B. Nonstationary Markov Games / C. R-MADDPG]_

"In our previous work [40], a variant of the partially observable Markov game, namely a nonstationary Markov game, is proposed to describe real-world situations better, i.e., G := ⟨N, S, {Ai}i∈N , {Oi}i∈N , {Ri}i∈N , T, U, γ⟩ where U is a set of domain randomization parameters that parameterizes the randomization environment based on prior knowledge."

"Recurrent multi-agent deep deterministic policy gradient (R-MADDPG) [41] is an extension of the multi-agent deep deterministic policy gradient (MADDPG) [42] by improving the actor-network and critic-network of MADDPG. It is superior in addressing the challenges posed by partially observable environments and limited communications."
