## Taxonomy
- **Robustness / perturbation type targeted**: Communication-centric perturbations — message perturbations/corruption (noise, loss, jumble, adversarial tampering), observation/state perturbations, transmission delays and asynchrony, limited bandwidth, Byzantine/faulty or non-cooperative agents, spoofing/jamming, privacy leakage. (Survey scope spanning robustness and efficiency of inter-agent communication.)
- **Method paradigm**: Survey / taxonomy. Covers reviewed paradigms: randomized smoothing / certified robustness, robust Markov game equilibria, adversarial training, game-theoretic (zero-sum attacker-defender) defenses, information-bottleneck regularization, message filtering/reconstruction, reliability-weighted aggregation, event-triggered / scheduling / gating, delay-aware learning, federated MARL.
- **Keywords**: robust communication, communication-efficient MARL, message perturbation, communication delay, limited bandwidth, certified/Byzantine-resilient defense

## TL;DR
A survey that systematically reviews robust and efficient communication strategies for MARL under realistic, non-ideal channel conditions (message perturbations, transmission delays, limited bandwidth), organizes the methods into a unified taxonomy, surveys three application domains (cooperative autonomous driving, distributed SLAM, federated learning), and outlines open challenges advocating a co-design of communication, learning, and robustness.

## Problem & Motivation
Most existing MARL approaches assume communication is instantaneous, reliable, and of unlimited bandwidth — conditions rarely met in real-world deployments. Real communication links are affected by noise and adversarial attacks, suffer unpredictable delays/losses/asynchronous arrivals, and are constrained by severe bandwidth limits; these imperfections destabilize learning, disrupt coordination, and can cause system failures, creating a gap between theoretical MARL models and practice. Prior surveys focus on open-environment coordination, general algorithmic paradigms, distributed training frameworks, and broad adversarial robustness, but lack a systematic treatment of MARL communication under realistic constraints (message corruption, transmission delays, bandwidth limitations). This survey fills that gap with a focused review of how communication is modeled, analyzed, and secured against practical impediments.

## Robustness Setting
- **Threat model / uncertainty set**: Multiple, surveyed across reviewed works — observation/state perturbations (sensor noise, measurement error, malicious attacks); message perturbations modeled probabilistically (e.g., loss with probability Ploss, Gaussian interference N(0,Vnoise), message jumble with probability Pjumble; arbitrary unbounded substitution with probability p as in ADMAC; lp-norm bounded additive perturbations and convex-combination attacks; BSC/AWGN/Bursty-Noise channel models); graph-topology and model-parameter perturbations; Byzantine/non-cooperative agents (faulty, naive, cautious, omniscient); spoofing/jamming; communication delays (fixed and time-varying/Gaussian) and asynchrony/missing data; bandwidth budgets.
- **Setting**: Primarily cooperative MARL (Dec-POMDP / MA-POMDP), some competitive/mixed and adversarial (zero-sum attacker-defender games); largely CTDE; online learning, with offline aggregation noted; communication channel often folded into environment dynamics.

## Method
- Establishes problem representations: Markov games (stochastic games) and the Dec-POMDP tuple ⟨N,S,A,P,R,O,Ω,γ⟩ with the global-return objective J(π) (Eq. 1), used as the common modeling foundation.
- Robustness axis (Section III.A): organizes defenses against (1) observation/state perturbations (e.g., CertifyCMARL randomized smoothing; robust equilibria with RMAQ/RMAAC; robust mean-field actor-critic; stochastic adversarial training), (2) message perturbations (TMC temporal message control with broadcast condition ‖mᵗᵢ−mᵗ⁻¹ᵢ‖₂>δ; noisy-channel MA-POMDP with BSC/AWGN/BN models; GP-based message filtering; ADMAC active defense with a reliability estimator and decomposable aggregation policy; R-MACRL two-player zero-sum filtering; MA3C auxiliary adversaries; AME ablated message ensemble with Byzantine-resilience guarantees; CroMAC interval-bound-propagation certification), and (3) other perturbations (graph-information-bottleneck MAGI; moving-target defense).
- Bandwidth axis (Section III.B + Section V): taxonomizes "who/when to communicate" (scheduling SchedNet, gating GACML/VBC/TMC, event-triggered ETCNet, topology ExpoComm) vs. "what/rate to communicate" (NDQ MI minimization, autoencoder/DVQ compression, personalized MAIC/PMAC/CACOM, consensus COCOM).
- Delay axis (Section IV): fixed-delay value-factorization with GRU history encoding (VFFAC), delay-tolerant Dec-POMDP (CoDe DT-Dec-POMDP), MAAMIF for missing/asynchronous info, and delay-aware scheduling (DACOM TimeNet, DAMARL, DAMIAN).
- Applications (Section VI): cooperative autonomous driving, distributed SLAM, and federated MARL, framed around latency/reliability, bandwidth, and communication–convergence–privacy trade-offs.

## Theoretical Contributions
None / mostly a survey. It synthesizes theoretical guarantees from reviewed works (e.g., existence of robust equilibria, AME's Byzantine-resilient certified guarantees when fewer than half of agents are compromised, CroMAC interval-bound-propagation certificates, certified team-reward lower bounds, federated MARL convergence bounds under periodic averaging) rather than proving new results.

## Experiments
- **Environment/Benchmark**: None of its own (survey). Reports benchmarks used by surveyed works, e.g., StarCraft micromanagement (SMAC), MPE, collaborative-perception benchmarks, Replica dataset (SLAM), vehicular/UAV/satellite-network and federated-learning settings.
- **Baselines**: Not applicable (survey).
- **Evaluation metrics**: Discusses how robustness is quantified in communicative MARL — winning rates, accumulated rewards, completion timesteps under various attack intensities; worst-case-attack utility (R-MACRL); certified team-reward lower bounds (CertifyCMARL); generalized robustness via transfer to unseen perturbation types.

## Key Results
- Provides a structured taxonomy of robust/efficient communication: separating robustness (observation/state, message, and structural/parameter perturbations) from efficiency (limited bandwidth, delays), and "who/when" vs. "what/rate" communication levers (Tables I–V; Figs. 1–4).
- Synthesizes the field's trajectory: from early differentiable protocols (RIAL/DIAL) toward scheduling/pruning, information-theoretic compression, event-triggered constrained optimization, personalized/context-aware protocols, scalable topologies, and from empirical defenses toward certified/Byzantine-resilient and game-theoretic robustness.
- Identifies open challenges: certifying performance under extreme rate limits and lossy channels; unifying efficiency with robustness to corrupted messages; coping with hybrid co-occurring threats (e.g., fading + jamming + deception); handling asynchronous, out-of-order, lossy delays; and reconciling communication efficiency with convergence guarantees and privacy in federated MARL — advocating a unified co-design of communication, learning, and robustness.

## Limitations & Future Work
- As a survey it contributes no new algorithm or empirical evaluation; it relies on synthesizing prior work.
- Future directions it proposes: embed intrinsic robustness into protocol design (information-bottleneck regularization combined with certifiable guarantees) and adaptive defenses that recognize and switch against ongoing corruption types; richer adversarial benchmarks unifying noise, erasure, jamming, and semantic manipulation; integrate intention prediction with temporal alignment for asynchronous delays; treat communication as an explicit resource-constrained action with semantic/phase-adaptive protocols; cross-layer, trust-aware and Byzantine-resilient federated MARL; and large-model-driven semantic communication with interpretability/auditing.

## Relevance to Survey
Highly relevant: this is itself a survey of robust (and efficient) MARL communication, providing a ready-made map of the communication-attack/defense sub-area of robust MARL. It connects directly to the survey's themes of communication robustness (message perturbations, adversarial attacks, Byzantine/fault tolerance), state/observation-perturbation robustness, safety, and the broader robust-MARL/robust-MDP/adversarial-RL lineage (e.g., SA-MDP, robust Markov games with state uncertainty, zero-sum attacker-defender formulations). It is a strong source of pointers and a taxonomy backbone for the communication-robustness chapter of the survey.

## Related Work (verbatim excerpts from the paper)

> _[Section III.A, "Robust MARL with unreliable inputs and communication" — introduction]_

"Reliable communication is imperative for facilitating collaborative decision-making and learning in multi-agent systems (MASs). However, real-world communication channels are frequently vulnerable to various forms of interference and adversarial attacks, including observation noise, message perturbations, malicious tampering, structural disruptions and so on. While existing research has extensively studied the robustness of agent policies against adversarial perturbations in state and action spaces [22], the vulnerabilities specifically inherent in communication mechanisms have received comparatively less attention. Consequently, these issues pose substantial threats to the robustness and reliability of MASs."

> _[Section III.A.1, "Perturbations on observations/states"]_

"In real-world MAS deployments, agents' observations and states are often disturbed by sensor noise [23], measurement errors, environmental uncertainties, or even malicious attacks. These disturbances can cause agents to make decisions based on inaccurate or misleading information, leading to serious consequences including policy failures, collaboration breakdowns, performance degradation, and security risks. For example, in a fleet of autonomous vehicles, misjudging the location of surrounding vehicles could result in collisions. To ensure the reliable application of MARL in realistic and complex environments, it is necessary to develop robust methods against observation and state disturbances. Mu et al. [24] first introduce provable robustness into cooperative MARL through the CertifyCMARL framework. Their method leverages randomized smoothing to certify perturbation bounds for agent actions and applies false discovery rate control to address the multi-agent authentication problem, thereby ensuring a global reward lower bound. He et al. [25] formalize state perturbations within a Markov game with adversaries, introducing and proving the existence of a robust equilibrium. They further propose practical algorithms RMAQ and RMAAC for learning robust policies under state uncertainty. Zhou et al. [26] propose a robust mean-field actor-critic method to defend against adversarial perturbations on agent states. They introduce a state-adversarial stochastic game framework along with a repetitive regularization technique to ensure robustness without degrading performance in clean environments. Zhou et al. [27] design a stochastic adversarial training framework to prevent overfitting to deterministic attacks. The framework combines a stochastic director for policy advice and a generator for observation perturbations, enhancing robustness without degrading clean performance. Although the above studies do not explicitly address communication architecture or secure content transmission in MASs, their in-depth analyses of observation and state perturbations provide valuable insights. The theoretical tools and algorithmic paradigms they develop provide a solid methodological foundation for future research on MARL robustness under communication interference."

> _[Section III.A.2, "Perturbations on messages"]_

"Reliable communication is crucial for effective multi-agent cooperative decision-making and learning. However, in real-world environments, messages are often disturbed by noise, loss, malicious attacks, and other factors. These perturbations poses serious challenges to the robustness of the system. Consequently, recent research has focused on enhancing the robustness of MASs, including channel modeling, message filtering, active evaluation, and provable defense."

> _[Section III.A.2, continued]_

"While exploring how agents learn effective action strategies and communication protocols, A3C2 [9] evaluated robustness under three kinds of noisy communication channels: message loss, external interference, and message jumble. Specifically, message loss is modeled by assigning a probability Ploss to each transmitted message. External interference is modeled by adding Gaussian noise N (0,Vnoise) to the received messages. Message jumble happens when received messages have a certain probability Pjumble of being mixed with others. In this case, agents receive the sum of all other agents' messages without being able to distinguish them individually."

> _[Section III.A.2, continued — ADMAC, R-MACRL, MA3C, AME, CroMAC]_

"Similarly, the active defense multi-agent communication (ADMAC [10]) framework is proposed to address the reliability of messages received by agents. Prior work mainly focused on passive defense strategies, where agents treated all messages equally, making it difficult to balance performance and robustness. ADMAC enables agents to actively evaluate the reliability of received messages. By incorporating a decomposable message aggregation policy network, ADMAC adjusts the impact of unreliable messages on the final decision, thereby adaptively reducing the influence of potentially malicious messages on the decision-making process."

"Lv et al. [31] propose a safe MARL framework to counter spoofing attacks that inject fake observations and learning parameters. The defense method combines a reputation mechanism for selecting reliable cooperating agents and a message authentication scheme that compares received data with local experience to filter out fake content. By ensuring only trustworthy information is used for policy updates, the method improves MARL safety and performance in adversarial wireless environments, such as anti-jamming video transmission."

"Because the above studies mainly focus on defense strategies under established attack patterns, Xue et al. [32] further systematically model the adversarial communication problem as a dynamic game between the attacker and defender. ... Many multi-agent communication and learning (MACAL) algorithms (e.g., CommNet [33], TarMAC [34], NDQ [35]) are vulnerable to learned adversarial message attacks. The authors propose a two-stage message filter consisting of an anomaly detector and a message reconstructor, which can identify malicious messages and recover their original contents, thus restoring the multi-agent cooperation performance under a variety of environments and algorithms. To address the limitations of static defenses, they formalize adversarial communication as a two-player zero-sum game and proposed the R-MACRL framework to approximate a Nash equilibrium policy. Similarly focusing on adversarial perturbations, Yuan et al. [36] propose multi-agent auxiliary adversaries generation for robust communication (MA3C) to train a robust policy by generating auxiliary attackers. They perturb every message received by each agent with an additive noise εi j, constrained within an lp-norm ball ... MA3C employs a population of diverse attackers during training, forcing the MAS to learn communication strategies that are resilient to a wide spectrum of message manipulations."

"In addition, several provable defense methods have been proposed from a theoretical perspective to enhance robustness in complex communication scenarios. For example, Sun et al. [37] introduce the ablated message ensemble (AME) framework, where randomized ablation of received messages allows agents to aggregate actions over multiple subsets. Under the condition that fewer than half of the agents are compromised, AME provides formal Byzantine-resilient guarantees for both single-step decisions and long-term returns. This certifiable approach departs from purely empirical defenses and provides theoretical guarantees against arbitrary corruption. Yuan et al. further explores the robustness of communication by proposing the CroMAC method [38]. They extract joint message representations using a multi-view variational autoencoder and provide theoretical robustness guarantees through interval bound propagation."

> _[Section III.A.3, "Other perturbations"]_

"Beyond direct perturbations on observations, actions, and messages, recent studies have identified more subtle attack surfaces in MASs, particularly within communication structures and model parameters. Ding et al. [39], [40] address the challenge of defending against perturbations in graph-based communication networks. They introduce the graph information bottleneck principle into MARL frameworks, proposing a novel approach called MAGI. It refines how agents process and share information by learning minimal sufficient message representations that maximize mutual information with optimal actions while minimizing dependence on vulnerable features. Through this dual-objective optimization, their framework effectively filters out noise and adversarial perturbations injected into either agent features or graph topology. In a different approach, Dong et al. [41] propose a model diversity-based moving target defense mechanism to counter adversarial perturbations in wireless communication systems."

> _[Section V.1, "Message-level compression and simplification" — robustness paragraph]_

"While compression and sparsification techniques enhance communication efficiency by reducing redundant transmissions, efficiency alone does not guarantee reliable coordination in realistic environments where messages are often corrupted, noisy, or adversarial. A complementary line of research therefore focuses on the robustness of communication, seeking to safeguard the semantic integrity of inter-agent messages under perturbations. Early explorations of robustness are grounded in the vulnerability of deep reinforcement learning policies to adversarial perturbations on state observations. The state-adversarial MDP formulation, together with theoretically principled policy regularization, is proposed to mitigate these vulnerabilities and improve robustness across control tasks [66]. Extending this perspective to the communication layer, differentiable communication learning is adapted to discrete noisy channels, introducing stochastic encoding and unbiased gradient estimation to handle unknown noise and thus narrow the gap between idealized analog communication and realistic digital channels [61]. Beyond signal-level robustness, emergent communication research shows that learning discrete tokens embedded in semantic spaces allows agents to develop semantically meaningful lexicons that remain effective under noise and even support zero-shot coordination with humans and unseen agents—significantly outperforming one-hot encodings in both resilience and generalization [67]."

> _[Section V.1, continued]_

"Building on this foundation, subsequent research explicitly investigates adversarial threats to communication. Systematic studies show that maliciously crafted messages destabilize coordination in MASs, motivating defense methods such as message reconstruction filters and game-theoretic training strategies, which improve worst-case robustness against learned attackers [32]. To provide formal guarantees, certifiable defenses are developed; for example, the ablated message ensemble framework aggregates decisions across multiple randomly ablated message subsets, ensuring that policies remain reliable even when a bounded fraction of communications are perturbed [37]. In parallel, active defense strategies are proposed, where agents dynamically evaluate the reliability of incoming messages based on their own observations and histories, and adjust the influence of suspicious communications on decision-making to achieve a controllable balance between accuracy and resilience [10]. To further strengthen defenses against diverse and adaptive adversaries, adversarial training frameworks introduce evolutionary or population-based auxiliary attackers, exposing agents to a wide spectrum of perturbations and enhancing generalization in unseen scenarios [36], [68]. Beyond adversarial modeling, information-theoretic methods reframe robustness as an inference problem: mutual information regularization acts as an information bottleneck, suppressing spurious correlations and aligning agent behaviors with robust action priors, thereby providing resilience even without explicit adversary exposure [69]. Most recently, robustness is considered at the protocol level. The imperfect byzantine generals problem (IBGP) formulation relaxes the requirement of full consensus, enabling partial agreement among benign agents and ensuring zero-shot resilience in heterogeneous and large-scale MASs [70]."
